# Atlas: Paper Map Driving

Atlas draws Southern California the way a printed road atlas does: cream paper, sepia roads,
hatched water and a vermilion route. Underneath it's a working navigation app for iPhone, with
search, a choice of routes and spoken turn-by-turn guidance, built on OpenStreetMap.

Coming soon to the App Store, free, for iPhone with iOS 17 or later.

## Where it works

The Los Angeles basin and Orange County down to San Diego and the border, east to San
Bernardino, Riverside and Temecula. Outside that area the map is blank and routes aren't
available.

## Find a place

- Search places and addresses as you type, with nearer matches first.
- See what a place is, whether it's open now, and its address, hours, phone and website, when
  OpenStreetMap has them.
- Or long-press anywhere on the map.

## Pick a route

- Up to two routes, each with its time, distance and arrival time, the fastest marked.
- Avoid toll roads, highways or ferries wherever there's another way.

## Drive

- A heading-up map with the next turn in a high-contrast banner: the distance to it and the
  street.
- Lane guidance where the road data has it, and the speed limit where it's known.
- Spoken instructions, with a mute button on every screen.
- A new route if you leave the planned one.
- Guidance carries on with the screen locked or another app in front.

## Demo drive

Turn on Demo drive in Settings and Start drives the route by itself, so you can watch navigation
from your desk or from anywhere.

## Good to know

- The map is stored in the app, so it draws without a signal. Search and routes need a
  connection.
- Arrival times don't include live traffic.
- No account, no ads, no tracking. The [privacy policy](privacy.md) says what the app sends.
- Questions or problems: [support](support.md).

Keep your eyes on the road, and follow posted signs and local laws: road data can be out of
date.
